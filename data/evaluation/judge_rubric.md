Rate the candidate reply, not the person or the conversation's writing style.
The conversation is oldest first. The incoming message is from the peer;
the candidate reply is a selectable draft the user can choose or edit before sending.

Before scoring, check what the peer is doing (asking, informing, inviting,
declining, etc.) and what the conversation already establishes. Read the reply
literally. Do not invent unstated events or reinterpret explicit constraints solely
to excuse a contradiction or role reversal.
Distinguish responding to the message from merely mentioning the same topic.
In the explanation, identify any contradiction, reversed role or missed intent.

Plausible new details are allowed in selectable drafts: personal progress,
availability, preferences, room locations, deadlines and third-party approvals
need not have appeared earlier. Missing prior evidence alone is not a flaw.
Both "Halfway through mine" and "Finished mine" can answer a progress question
when the user's progress is unknown. Do not prefer an acceptance over a valid
refusal, or a bland clarification over a useful specific draft, without a clear
contextual or wording reason. Respect explicit facts and the latest agreed plan:
if the user cannot meet before eight, "Sure, six works" is contradictory.

Score each criterion independently using integers 1-5:

Appropriateness: does this reply work as the user's next turn?
5 = directly and correctly handles the message in context.
4 = fits well with only a minor omission.
3 = partly answers or is generic but still relevant to the message's purpose.
2 = mostly misses the intent or ignores important context.
1 = unrelated, nonsensical or contradicts an established fact.

Naturalness: does the wording sound like plausible casual texting?
5 = fluent and effortless; 4 = natural with a minor awkward bit;
3 = understandable but stiff or slightly odd; 2 = clearly awkward;
1 = unreadable, garbled or repetitive. Do not lower this solely because the
reply is contextually wrong. Informal spelling or lowercase alone is not a flaw.

Style fit: use the requested style, not the peer's style or the persona's habits.
STANDARD = plain, neutral English; ordinary casual phrasing is allowed.
No Singlish particles or local slang are required or rewarded.
LOCAL = recognizably natural Singapore English/Singlish, with enough local
phrasing, syntax, vocabulary or pragmatic particles to distinguish it from
ordinary STANDARD English. Nearly neutral English alone does not earn top
LOCAL style marks. Do not count or stack particles, force slang or reward
invented Mandarin. Minor politeness differences are acceptable.
5 = matches the requested style; 4 = minor register slip; 3 = borderline;
2 = clearly wrong register or forced local phrasing; 1 = opposite register or
offensive slang. A wrong answer can still score highly for style fit.

Reference-wording agreement is not a quality criterion: a different reply may
be fully appropriate. This task rates one draft only; it cannot establish whether
the STANDARD and LOCAL drafts preserve approximately the same meaning. That
requires a separate paired review with both drafts visible.

Would-send is a separate decision: true only if this reply works in context
and could be sent with little or no editing if the user selects that draft's
plausible details as applicable. This does not certify unknown personal facts
as true. Fluent wording alone is not enough.
Return a short explanation grounded in the supplied text. Use uncertain=true
if you cannot confidently interpret the language or context; do not invent certainty.
Keep the same anchors for every fictional persona. Do not rewrite the reply.
